# Pricing & Commercials

> All figures below are **initial pricing hypotheses in EGP**, not verified market averages or approved final prices. Validate them through real proposals, delivery effort, customer budgets, and margin results.

## Early pricing hypotheses

| Offer | Initial test range | Important qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| Limited business/technical audit | EGP 5,000–12,000 | Fixed duration and defined deliverable |
| Small company website | EGP 20,000–45,000 | Content, integrations, e-commerce, and migration affect scope |
| Initial custom business system | EGP 40,000–100,000 | Only for tightly bounded MVP scope |
| Limited monthly care plan | EGP 5,000–15,000/month | Defined hours, response window, and exclusions |

Complex systems, extensive integrations, mobile apps, data migration, urgent deadlines, and international contracts need separate estimates.

## Build an estimate

Include:
- Discovery and requirements.
- Design and implementation.
- Testing, deployment, and documentation.
- Project communication and coordination.
- Risks, unknowns, and contingency.
- Third-party fees and recurring infrastructure.
- Post-launch support obligations.
- Taxes and payment-processing or foreign-exchange costs where applicable.

Never price solely by coding hours without accounting for delivery risk and ongoing responsibility.

## Proposal structure

1. Customer context and desired outcome.
2. Scope and deliverables.
3. Explicit exclusions and assumptions.
4. Milestones and acceptance criteria.
5. Timeline dependencies and customer responsibilities.
6. Price, currency, tax treatment, and payment schedule.
7. Change request rates and approval process.
8. Warranty, maintenance, and support options.
9. Quote validity and signature/approval steps.

## Payment principles

Use an advance payment and milestone-based instalments that match the risk and workload. The exact percentages are deal-specific and must be written in the signed agreement. Confirm each milestone payment before beginning work that depends on it; never treat an issued invoice as collected cash.

For overseas clients, clarify currency, fees, exchange-rate risk, transfer method, applicable taxes, and payment timing in writing.

## Review after each project

Compare estimated effort with actual effort, delivery cost, unplanned work, collection delay, and client value. Adjust the next quote using evidence. Do not lower price to win work if the scope and delivery obligations remain unchanged.
